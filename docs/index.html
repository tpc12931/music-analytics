Can audio characteristics explain which songs become Billboard hits?

Exploring the differences between historic hits and non-hits, and the trajectories of songs after they enter the Hot 100. This page recreates data visualizations with embedded SVG and JavaScript—no external chart libraries.

Expanded published source911,027Track records across two publisher files
Approved model sample71,411After approved 5:1 sampling and missing-value handling
Binary model accuracy83.25%Equal to majority-class baseline · no lift
Read this first: All model statistics are in-sample and retrospective. The retrospective hit label indicates Billboard Hot 100 entry—not a streaming “flop,” nor a validated release-day prediction. The approved 5:1 sample has a synthetic 16.7% hit prevalence, not a population probability. Spotify popularity is a snapshot collected after release.

Source: MusicOSet original and expanded datasets, Silva et al., https://marianaossilva.github.io/DSW2019/updates.html. Expanded source: `hits_dataset.csv` and `nonhits_dataset.csv`; chart history: `song_chart.csv` from publisher archive. No raw song data are embedded beyond an anonymized sample of numeric feature values.

Limitations: Non-hits were sampled from artists with Billboard hits; no broad-market representativeness claim. The popularity field represents a later snapshot; its inclusion as a hit predictor is excluded to prevent leakage. The primary models were fit and evaluated in-sample as specified by the course. Chart presence does not imply zero streams outside the chart. Cleaning, sampling, and model selections were approved by the student on November 18, 2026. This is not validation of future forecasting, nor independent instructor acceptance.
